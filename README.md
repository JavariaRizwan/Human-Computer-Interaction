## Introduction

For this assignment, I explored Figma and selected a Student Portal
Dashboard template from the Figma Community. I reviewed the interface to
identify possible usability problems and considered how they could be
improved.

I chose this design because a student portal is something students may
use regularly to check courses, payments, results, notices and
schedules. This makes it a useful example for looking at whether
information is easy to find, understand and use.

After reviewing the dashboard, I focused on the **Payment Info** section
and created a separate payment information screen to make fee details
clearer.

## How I checked it

I opened the selected design in Figma and looked through the dashboard
elements. I considered the readability of text, colour contrast,
navigation labels, information hierarchy, spacing and whether the
purpose of each element was clear.

I used basic HCI and usability ideas discussed in class, such as
visibility, consistency, readability, recognition rather than recall and
ease of navigation.

This was an initial visual inspection. The problems below are my
observations and have not yet been confirmed through formal user
testing.

## Problems I found

### Dashboard

**1. Low contrast in the sidebar navigation**

Some inactive navigation labels use a light colour on the purple
sidebar. This may make them difficult to read, particularly for users
with low vision or when viewing the screen on a display with poor
brightness.

**Suggested fix:** Use a darker or brighter text colour with stronger
contrast against the sidebar background. The selected navigation item
should also be clearly distinguishable from the inactive items.

**2. Search field does not explain what can be searched**

The search field only displays the word "Search". It does not tell
students whether they can search for courses, notices, instructors or
other information.

**Suggested fix:** Use a more descriptive placeholder, such as "Search
courses, notices..." so students have a better idea of what the search
field is for.

**3. Instructor photos do not provide enough information**

The dashboard displays instructor photos, but their names and the
courses they teach are not visible.

**Suggested fix:** Add each instructor's name and, where space allows,
the related course or subject. This would make the profiles more useful.

**4. Daily notices are not prominent enough**

The daily notice area contains information about payment deadlines and
examination schedules, but the text is relatively small and the
important details do not stand out clearly.

**Suggested fix:** Make notice headings and dates easier to scan.
Important or urgent notices could use clear priority labels, while
keeping the text readable.

**5. Finance summary does not clearly show the remaining amount**

The dashboard displays "Total Payable", "Total Paid" and "Others".
However, it does not directly show how much the student still needs to
pay. The meaning of "Others" is also not very clear.

**Suggested fix:** Show the outstanding or remaining balance directly
and use clear labels for each financial amount. A separate Payment Info
screen can provide more detailed information.

## The one I improved in Figma: Payment Info

I focused on the finance and payment issue and created a separate
**Payment Info** screen. I wanted students to be able to understand
their fee situation without having to calculate the remaining amount
themselves.

The new screen includes the following improvements:

**1. Separate payment summary cards**

I used three cards to show:

-   **Total Fee:** The complete fee amount.
-   **Paid:** The amount the student has already paid.
-   **Remaining:** The amount still to be paid.

This makes the outstanding balance easier to notice.

**2. Payment progress bar**

I added a progress indicator to show how much of the total fee has been
paid. The percentage and amounts provide extra information alongside the
visual bar.

**3. Payment history**

I added a transaction table with the payment date, description, amount
and status. This gives students a place to review their payment records.

**4. Clear payment statuses**

I used labels such as "Paid", "Pending" and "Overdue" to make
transaction statuses easier to understand. The status is written as text
rather than being communicated only through colour.

**5. Payment actions**

I included clearly named actions such as "Pay Now", "Download Receipt"
and "View Invoice". These make common payment-related tasks easier to
identify. They are prototype actions and do not process real payments or
generate actual receipts unless connected to a working service.

**6. Navigation between screens**

I connected the Payment Info option on the dashboard to the new Payment
Info screen in Figma Prototype mode. I also planned a Dashboard link on
the payment screen so users can return to the main dashboard.

The payment amounts and transaction records in the design are sample
data for demonstration only.

## What I would improve next

The Payment Info screen is the main part I adapted for this assignment.
The other issues are recorded as possible improvements for future
iterations.

If I continue working on the dashboard, I would prioritise:

1.  Improving the contrast and readability of sidebar labels.
2.  Making the search field more descriptive.
3.  Adding instructor names and course information.
4.  Making important notices and deadlines easier to notice.
5.  Reviewing the design with students to see whether the changes
    actually help them complete common tasks.

## Conclusion

The selected Student Portal Dashboard has a clean purple-and-white
appearance and brings several student services together in one place.
However, my review highlighted some areas that could be clearer,
including sidebar readability, search guidance, instructor
identification, notice visibility and the presentation of payment
information.

For my first improvement, I created a dedicated Payment Info screen with
clearer fee summaries, a payment progress indicator, payment history and
transaction statuses. I also connected it to the dashboard in the Figma
prototype.

This activity helped me understand that a good interface is not only
about appearance. It should also make information easy to find, make
actions understandable and help users complete their tasks with less
confusion.
